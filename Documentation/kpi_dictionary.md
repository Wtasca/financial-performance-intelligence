## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the standard definitions, calculation rules and data requirements for the financial KPIs used within the Northbridge Building Society Financial Performance & Profitability Intelligence solution.

The framework aims to:

- Establish consistent financial metric definitions across the organisation.
- Support accurate financial performance reporting.
- Compare actual financial performance against budget.
- Monitor income, expenditure and profitability.
- Analyse the financial contribution of mortgage and savings products.
- Improve visibility of departmental spending.
- Support cash flow monitoring and financial forecasting.
- Provide a reliable foundation for SQL development and Power BI reporting.

All figures and examples used in this project are synthetic and illustrative.

## 2. General Calculation Principles

### 2.1 Reporting conventions

| Attribute | Standard |
|---|---|
| Currency | GBP (£) |
| Reporting frequency | Monthly |
| Financial year | 1 April – 31 March |
| Historical reporting period | Three complete financial years |
| Default reporting basis | Monthly and year-to-date |
| Financial values | Stored as decimal numbers |
| Percentage values | Calculated from underlying values, not averaged |
| Transaction dates | Based on accounting/posting date |
| Reporting periods | Based on financial calendar |

### 2.2 Financial year definition

Northbridge operates an April–March financial year.

For example:

- FY2025: April 2024 – March 2025.
- FY2026: April 2025 – March 2026.
- FY2027: April 2026 – March 2027.

The reporting model must distinguish between calendar year and financial year.

### 2.3 Aggregation principles

Financial measures must be aggregated according to their underlying nature.

| Measure type | Aggregation rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Financial transactions | Sum | Interest income |
| Monthly budgets | Sum across periods | Annual department budget |
| Portfolio balances | Average or period-end snapshot | Mortgage balances |
| Percentages and ratios | Recalculate from aggregated numerator and denominator | Cost-to-Income Ratio |
| Headcount | Use period-end or average headcount, depending on metric | Cost per Employee |
| Growth | Compare equivalent reporting periods | Revenue Growth |

**Important:** Ratios, margins and percentages should not normally be averaged across months or departments. They must be recalculated using the relevant aggregated values.

For example, the annual Cost-to-Income Ratio is calculated as total annual operating expenses divided by total annual income, not the average of the twelve monthly ratios.

### 2.4 Sign conventions

The following conventions apply:

- Income and revenue are recorded as positive values.
- Expenses are recorded as positive values in the reporting layer.
- Interest expense is recorded as a positive expense value.
- Net Interest Income is calculated by subtracting interest expense from interest income.
- Impairment charges are recorded as positive expenses.
- Cash inflows and outflows are stored separately as positive amounts.

The reporting layer must avoid inconsistent signs between source systems.

## 3. KPI Category A – Income and Revenue

### KPI A01 – Total Income

**Definition:**  
The total income generated by Northbridge during a reporting period, including interest income and non-interest income.

**Formula:**

Total Income = Interest Income + Non-interest Income

**Data requirements:**
- Transaction ID
- Posting Date
- Financial Category
- Income Type
- Department ID
- Product ID
- Amount

**Source data:** General Ledger.

**Aggregation:** Sum all qualifying income transactions within the selected reporting period.

**Frequency:** Monthly, quarterly and annually.

**Example:**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Interest income | £6,000,000 |
| Non-interest income | £450,000 |
| Total income | £6,450,000 |

**Business interpretation:**  
Provides an overall view of income generation. Management can monitor whether income is increasing or decreasing and identify the main sources of income.

**Reporting considerations:**  
Income classifications must be mutually exclusive to prevent interest income being counted twice.

### KPI A02 – Interest Income

**Definition:**  
Income earned from interest-bearing assets, primarily mortgage lending and other interest-earning investments.

**Formula:**

Interest Income = Sum of qualifying interest income transactions

**Data requirements:**
- Posting Date
- Product ID
- Department ID
- Financial Category
- Amount

**Source data:** General Ledger and product-level financial reporting.

**Aggregation:** Sum within the selected period, with optional breakdown by product category.

**Example:**  
Mortgage interest income of £5,200,000 and other interest income of £800,000 result in total interest income of £6,000,000.

**Business interpretation:**  
An increase may indicate portfolio growth, higher interest rates or a change in product mix. It should be assessed alongside interest expense and average earning assets.

### KPI A03 – Interest Expense

**Definition:**  
The interest cost incurred by Northbridge to fund its lending and other interest-bearing activities, primarily through savings deposits.

**Formula:**

Interest Expense = Sum of qualifying interest expense transactions

**Data requirements:**
- Posting Date
- Product ID
- Department ID
- Financial Category
- Amount

**Source data:** General Ledger and savings portfolio reporting.

**Aggregation:** Sum within the reporting period.

**Example:**  
Savings interest expense of £3,800,000 during a month.

**Business interpretation:**  
An increase may reflect higher savings rates, increased deposit balances or a change in the savings product mix. Management should compare this metric with interest income to assess its effect on the net interest margin.

### KPI A04 – Net Interest Income (NII)

**Definition:**  
The difference between interest earned on assets and interest paid on funding.

**Formula:**

NII = Interest Income − Interest Expense

**Data requirements:**
- Interest income
- Interest expense
- Posting Date
- Product ID
- Department ID

**Source data:** General Ledger and product financial reporting.

**Aggregation:** Calculate using the total interest income and total interest expense for the selected period.

**Example:**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Interest income | £6,000,000 |
| Interest expense | £3,800,000 |
| Net Interest Income | £2,200,000 |

**Business interpretation:**  
NII is a key indicator of the financial performance of a building society's core lending and deposit-taking activities.

A declining NII may indicate that funding costs are increasing faster than lending income, or that the product mix is becoming less favourable.

### KPI A05 – Non-interest Income

**Definition:**  
Income generated from sources other than interest, including fees, commissions and other qualifying operating income.

**Formula:**

Non-interest Income = Sum of qualifying non-interest income transactions

**Data requirements:**
- Posting Date
- Income Category
- Department ID
- Product ID, where applicable
- Amount

**Source data:** General Ledger.

**Aggregation:** Sum within the reporting period.

**Example:**  
Fee and commission income of £450,000 during a month.

**Business interpretation:**  
Helps management understand the contribution of non-interest income and assess the diversity of the organisation's income sources.

### KPI A06 – Income Growth

**Definition:**  
The percentage change in total income compared with an equivalent previous reporting period.

**Formula:**

Income Growth % = ((Current Period Income − Previous Period Income) / Previous Period Income) × 100

**Data requirements:**
- Reporting Period
- Total Income

**Source data:** Derived from General Ledger reporting.

**Aggregation:** Recalculate from the two comparable period totals.

**Example:**

| Period | Total income |
|---|---:|
| Previous month | £6,000,000 |
| Current month | £6,450,000 |

Income Growth = 7.5%

**Business interpretation:**  
Shows whether the organisation is generating more or less income over time.

**Reporting considerations:**
- Allow month-on-month, quarter-on-quarter and year-on-year comparisons.
- Compare equivalent periods.
- If the previous period is zero, return blank or not applicable rather than an artificial percentage.
- Distinguish between growth caused by portfolio expansion and growth caused by pricing or interest rates.

### KPI A07 – Year-to-Date Income

**Definition:**  
Cumulative total income generated from the beginning of the financial year up to the selected reporting period.

**Formula:**

YTD Income = Sum of monthly Total Income from April to the selected month.

**Data requirements:**
- Posting Date
- Financial Year
- Financial Period
- Total Income

**Source data:** Derived from General Ledger.

**Aggregation:** Cumulative sum within the current financial year.

**Example:**  
If total income from April to June is £18,900,000, the June YTD Income is £18,900,000.

**Business interpretation:**  
Allows management to monitor financial performance against the annual plan and compare cumulative performance with previous financial years.

## 4. KPI Category B – Profitability and Efficiency

### KPI B01 – Operating Profit

**Definition:**  
The financial result generated from operating activities after deducting operating expenses and impairment charges from total income.

**Formula:**

Operating Profit = Total Income − Operating Expenses − Impairment Charges

**Data requirements:**
- Total Income
- Operating Expenses
- Impairment Charges
- Reporting Period

**Source data:** General Ledger.

**Aggregation:** Calculate from the aggregated components within the selected reporting period.

**Example:**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Total income | £6,450,000 |
| Operating expenses | £4,000,000 |
| Impairment charges | £150,000 |
| Operating profit | £2,300,000 |

**Business interpretation:**  
Provides an overview of the organisation's ability to generate profit after covering its operating costs and recognised impairment charges.

**Reporting considerations:**  
This is a project-defined management measure. Its classification should be reconciled to the organisation's formal financial reporting policies before being treated as a statutory profit measure.

### KPI B02 – Operating Profit Margin

**Definition:**  
The percentage of total income retained as operating profit after operating expenses and impairment charges.

**Formula:**

Operating Profit Margin % = (Operating Profit / Total Income) × 100

**Data requirements:**
- Operating Profit
- Total Income

**Source data:** Derived from General Ledger.

**Aggregation:** Recalculate using aggregated operating profit and total income.

**Example:**

Operating Profit = £2,300,000  
Total Income = £6,450,000

Operating Profit Margin = 35.66%

**Business interpretation:**  
A higher margin generally indicates that more income is being retained after operating costs and impairment charges.

### KPI B03 – Net Interest Margin (NIM)

**Definition:**  
Measures the net interest income generated relative to average interest-earning assets.

**Formula:**

NIM % = (Annualised Net Interest Income / Average Interest-Earning Assets) × 100

For monthly reporting:

Monthly NIM % = (Monthly NII × 12 / Average Interest-Earning Assets) × 100

**Data requirements:**
- Monthly Interest Income
- Monthly Interest Expense
- Average interest-earning assets
- Reporting Period

**Source data:** General Ledger and mortgage/other interest-earning asset portfolio snapshots.

**Aggregation:** Calculate using the total NII and average interest-earning assets for the selected period. Do not average monthly NIM percentages to produce an annual NIM.

**Example:**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Monthly NII | £2,200,000 |
| Average interest-earning assets | £1,500,000,000 |
| Annualisation factor | 12 |
| Annualised NII | £26,400,000 |
| NIM | 1.76% |

**Business interpretation:**  
NIM is a key measure of the effectiveness of the organisation's lending and funding activities.

A declining NIM may indicate increasing funding costs, lower lending yields or a less profitable portfolio mix.

**Reporting considerations:**
- Define interest-earning assets consistently.
- Do not automatically use total assets as the denominator.
- Prefer average daily balances where available.
- If only monthly snapshots are available, use a documented average-balance methodology.
- For a multi-month reporting period, annualise NII using the number of days in the period relative to the financial year convention, or use a consistent monthly annualisation factor for monthly reporting.

### KPI B04 – Operating Expenses

**Definition:**  
Total costs incurred in running the organisation, excluding interest expense and other costs classified separately.

**Formula:**

Operating Expenses = Sum of qualifying operating expense transactions

**Data requirements:**
- Posting Date
- Expense Category
- Department ID
- Cost Centre ID
- Amount

**Source data:** General Ledger.

**Aggregation:** Sum within the selected reporting period.

**Example:**

| Expense category | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Personnel | £2,000,000 |
| Technology | £700,000 |
| Premises | £500,000 |
| Marketing | £300,000 |
| Professional services | £500,000 |
| Total operating expenses | £4,000,000 |

**Business interpretation:**  
Provides visibility of the organisation's cost base and allows management to identify major expense categories and departments.

**Reporting considerations:**  
Interest expense must not be included in operating expenses for this framework because it is already accounted for in NII.

### KPI B05 – Cost-to-Income Ratio (CIR)

**Definition:**  
Measures operating expenses as a proportion of total income.

**Formula:**

CIR % = (Operating Expenses / Total Income) × 100

**Data requirements:**
- Operating Expenses
- Total Income

**Source data:** Derived from General Ledger.

**Aggregation:** Recalculate using total operating expenses divided by total income for the selected period.

**Example:**

Operating Expenses = £4,000,000  
Total Income = £6,450,000

CIR = 62.02%

**Business interpretation:**  
A lower CIR generally indicates better operating cost efficiency, while a higher ratio may indicate that costs are increasing faster than income.

**Reporting considerations:**  
The project will use operating expenses divided by total income as its standard management definition. Any differences from externally published industry definitions must be documented before making external comparisons.

### KPI B06 – Personnel Costs

**Definition:**  
Total employee-related expenditure incurred during the reporting period.

**Formula:**

Personnel Costs = Sum of qualifying personnel expense transactions

**Data requirements:**
- Posting Date
- Department ID
- Expense Category
- Amount

**Source data:** General Ledger.

**Aggregation:** Sum within the selected period.

**Example:**  
Monthly personnel costs of £2,000,000.

**Business interpretation:**  
Allows management to monitor staffing-related expenditure and identify departments with significant changes in personnel costs.

### KPI B07 – Technology Costs

**Definition:**  
Total expenditure associated with technology operations, infrastructure, software, IT services and related suppliers.

**Formula:**

Technology Costs = Sum of qualifying technology expense transactions

**Data requirements:**
- Posting Date
- Department ID
- Supplier or Cost Category
- Amount

**Source data:** General Ledger.

**Aggregation:** Sum within the selected period.

**Example:**  
Monthly technology expenditure of £700,000.

**Business interpretation:**  
Supports monitoring of technology spending, supplier costs and technology investment trends.

### KPI B08 – Cost per Employee

**Definition:**  
Average operating expenditure per employee.

**Formula:**

Cost per Employee = Operating Expenses / Average Headcount

**Data requirements:**
- Operating Expenses
- Department ID
- Reporting Period
- Headcount
- Headcount Date

**Source data:** General Ledger and synthetic HR/headcount dataset.

**Aggregation:** Divide the selected period's operating expenses by the corresponding average headcount. Do not sum or average monthly ratios for an annual result.

**Example:**

Operating Expenses = £4,000,000  
Average Headcount = 450

Cost per Employee = £8,888.89

**Business interpretation:**  
Provides a high-level view of operating expenditure relative to organisational size.

**Reporting considerations:**  
This is an organisation-level efficiency indicator, not an employee salary measure. It should be interpreted alongside organisational structure, outsourcing and changes in operating activity.

## 5. KPI Category C – Budget and Financial Control

### KPI C01 – Budget

**Definition:**  
The approved financial allocation for a department, cost centre or financial category over a specified reporting period.

**Formula:**

Budget = Sum of approved budget values

**Data requirements:**
- Budget ID
- Financial Year
- Financial Period
- Department ID
- Cost Centre ID
- Financial Category
- Budget Amount
- Budget Version or Status

**Source data:** Budget dataset.

**Aggregation:** Sum monthly budget values across the selected reporting period.

**Example:**  
Annual Technology Department budget = £8,400,000.

**Business interpretation:**  
Establishes the approved spending or income target against which actual financial performance is measured.

**Reporting considerations:**
- Use the approved budget version for the selected reporting period.
- Maintain separate versions if revised budgets or forecasts are introduced.
- Do not mix approved budgets with forecast values.

### KPI C02 – Actual vs Budget Variance

**Definition:**  
Measures the difference between actual financial performance and the approved budget.

**Formula:**

Raw Variance = Actual − Budget

**Data requirements:**
- Actual Amount
- Budget Amount
- Financial Category
- Department ID
- Cost Centre ID
- Reporting Period

**Source data:** General Ledger and Budget dataset.

**Aggregation:** Calculate actual and budget totals independently, then subtract.

**Example – Expenses:**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Actual expenditure | £750,000 |
| Budget expenditure | £700,000 |
| Raw variance | +£50,000 |

**Business interpretation:**  
A positive expense variance indicates overspending. A negative expense variance indicates underspending.

For income categories, the interpretation is reversed: a positive variance indicates income above budget.

**Important:**  
The raw variance must always remain Actual minus Budget. Favourable or unfavourable status must be determined separately using the financial category type.

### KPI C03 – Budget Variance Percentage

**Definition:**  
Measures the percentage difference between actual financial performance and budget.

**Formula:**

Variance % = ((Actual − Budget) / Budget) × 100

**Data requirements:**
- Actual Amount
- Budget Amount
- Financial Category
- Reporting Period

**Source data:** General Ledger and Budget dataset.

**Aggregation:** Recalculate from the aggregated actual and budget amounts.

**Example – Expenses:**

Actual = £750,000  
Budget = £700,000

Variance % = 7.14%

**Business interpretation:**  
The department has spent 7.14% more than its approved budget.

**Reporting considerations:**
- If budget is zero, return blank or not applicable rather than dividing by zero.
- A positive percentage is not automatically favourable or unfavourable. The financial category determines the interpretation.

### KPI C04 – Budget Utilisation

**Definition:**  
Measures the proportion of the approved budget that has been consumed.

**Formula:**

Budget Utilisation % = (Actual Expenditure / Approved Budget) × 100

**Data requirements:**
- Actual Expenditure
- Approved Budget
- Department ID
- Financial Period

**Source data:** General Ledger and Budget dataset.

**Aggregation:** Calculate using cumulative actual expenditure divided by cumulative approved budget for the selected period.

**Example:**

Actual expenditure = £750,000  
Budget = £700,000

Budget Utilisation = 107.14%

**Business interpretation:**  
Values above 100% indicate that actual expenditure has exceeded the approved budget.

**Reporting considerations:**  
Use this KPI for expenditure categories. Income performance should be assessed using actual versus budget variance instead.

### KPI C05 – Favourable / Unfavourable Variance

**Definition:**  
Classifies a financial variance according to whether it benefits or adversely affects financial performance.

**Calculation rules:**

For income:
- Actual greater than Budget = Favourable.
- Actual lower than Budget = Unfavourable.

For expenses:
- Actual lower than Budget = Favourable.
- Actual greater than Budget = Unfavourable.

**Data requirements:**
- Actual Amount
- Budget Amount
- Financial Category Type
- Reporting Period

**Source data:** General Ledger and Budget dataset.

**Aggregation:** Determine status after aggregating actual and budget amounts at the selected reporting level.

**Example:**

| Financial category | Actual | Budget | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Interest income | £6.2m | £6.0m | Favourable |
| Technology expenses | £750k | £700k | Unfavourable |
| Personnel expenses | £1.9m | £2.0m | Favourable |

**Business interpretation:**  
Provides management with a clear indication of where financial performance is ahead of or behind plan.

**Reporting considerations:**  
Favourability is a financial classification, not necessarily a statement of operational quality. Underspending may reflect efficiency, delayed investment or failure to deliver planned activity.

## 6. KPI Category D – Product Performance and Profitability

### KPI D01 – Average Mortgage Balance

**Definition:**  
The average outstanding mortgage balance over a selected reporting period.

**Formula:**

Average Mortgage Balance = Average of mortgage portfolio balances over the reporting period

**Data requirements:**
- Snapshot Date
- Product ID
- Mortgage Balance
- Product Category

**Source data:** Mortgage portfolio snapshots.

**Aggregation:** Prefer average daily balances. If only monthly snapshots are available, use a documented monthly average or opening/closing balance methodology.

**Example:**  
Opening balance = £1,490,000,000  
Closing balance = £1,510,000,000

Simple opening/closing average = £1,500,000,000.

**Business interpretation:**  
Provides insight into mortgage portfolio size and growth.

### KPI D02 – Average Savings Balance

**Definition:**  
The average savings deposit balance held by customers during a selected reporting period.

**Formula:**

Average Savings Balance = Average of savings portfolio balances over the reporting period

**Data requirements:**
- Snapshot Date
- Product ID
- Savings Balance
- Product Category

**Source data:** Savings portfolio snapshots.

**Aggregation:** Use the same average-balance approach consistently across all savings products.

**Example:**  
Opening savings balance = £1,680,000,000  
Closing savings balance = £1,720,000,000

Simple opening/closing average = £1,700,000,000.

**Business interpretation:**  
Helps management monitor funding levels, deposit growth and changes in the savings product mix.

### KPI D03 – Product Net Interest Income

**Definition:**  
Measures the net interest contribution of an individual mortgage or savings product.

**Formula:**

Product NII = Product Interest Income − Product Interest Expense

For lending products, interest income is the principal component. For savings products, interest expense is the principal component.

**Data requirements:**
- Reporting Period
- Product ID
- Interest Income
- Interest Expense
- Product Category

**Source data:** General Ledger and product-level financial reporting.

**Aggregation:** Sum interest income and interest expense independently by product and period, then calculate the difference.

**Example:**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Mortgage product interest income | £1,200,000 |
| Related interest expense | £650,000 |
| Product NII | £550,000 |

**Business interpretation:**  
Allows management to compare the interest contribution of different products and identify products with stronger or weaker funding economics.

**Reporting considerations:**  
Any allocation of funding costs to lending products must use a documented methodology. Do not assume that a product's gross interest income represents its profitability.

### KPI D04 – Product Net Interest Margin

**Definition:**  
Measures the net interest income generated by a product relative to its average interest-earning balance.

**Formula:**

Product NIM % = (Annualised Product NII / Average Product Interest-Earning Balance) × 100

**Data requirements:**
- Product ID
- Reporting Period
- Product NII
- Average Interest-Earning Balance

**Source data:** General Ledger and mortgage/product portfolio snapshots.

**Aggregation:** Recalculate using aggregated product NII and the relevant average balance.

**Example:**

Monthly product NII = £550,000  
Average product interest-earning balance = £400,000,000

Annualised Product NIM = (550,000 × 12 / 400,000,000) × 100 = 1.65%.

**Business interpretation:**  
Allows product managers to compare the interest margin generated by different lending products.

**Reporting considerations:**  
For savings products, a standalone product NIM may not be meaningful because the savings product represents a funding source rather than an interest-earning asset. Savings products should primarily be assessed through their funding cost and contribution to the overall funding mix.

### KPI D05 – Product Contribution

**Definition:**  
Measures the financial contribution generated by a product after deducting directly attributable product costs.

**Formula:**

Product Contribution = Product Income − Directly Attributable Product Costs

Where product income includes relevant interest and non-interest income, and direct costs include costs that can be reliably attributed to the product.

**Data requirements:**
- Product ID
- Reporting Period
- Product Income
- Direct Product Costs
- Cost Allocation Method, where applicable

**Source data:** General Ledger, product financial reporting and product cost allocation dataset.

**Aggregation:** Sum product income and direct costs separately, then calculate contribution.

**Example:**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Product income | £1,200,000 |
| Direct product costs | £250,000 |
| Product contribution | £950,000 |

**Business interpretation:**  
Helps management understand which products contribute most to the organisation's financial performance.

**Reporting considerations:**
- Keep direct costs separate from allocated overhead.
- Do not allocate organisation-wide overhead without a documented and consistently applied methodology.
- Product contribution is not the same as net profit.
- Product NII and product contribution should remain separate metrics.

## 7. KPI Category E – Cash Flow

### KPI E01 – Cash Inflows

**Definition:**  
Total cash received by Northbridge during a reporting period within the defined cash flow reporting scope.

**Formula:**

Cash Inflows = Sum of qualifying cash inflow transactions

**Data requirements:**
- Transaction ID
- Transaction Date
- Cash Flow Category
- Amount
- Cash Account ID

**Source data:** Synthetic cash flow dataset.

**Aggregation:** Sum qualifying inflows within the selected period.

**Example:**  
Monthly cash inflows = £120,000,000.

**Business interpretation:**  
Shows the volume and sources of cash received during the reporting period.

### KPI E02 – Cash Outflows

**Definition:**  
Total cash paid by Northbridge during a reporting period within the defined cash flow reporting scope.

**Formula:**

Cash Outflows = Sum of qualifying cash outflow transactions

**Data requirements:**
- Transaction ID
- Transaction Date
- Cash Flow Category
- Amount
- Cash Account ID

**Source data:** Synthetic cash flow dataset.

**Aggregation:** Sum qualifying outflows within the selected period.

**Example:**  
Monthly cash outflows = £115,000,000.

**Business interpretation:**  
Helps management understand the volume and composition of cash payments.

### KPI E03 – Net Cash Flow

**Definition:**  
The difference between cash inflows and cash outflows during a reporting period.

**Formula:**

Net Cash Flow = Cash Inflows − Cash Outflows

**Data requirements:**
- Cash Inflows
- Cash Outflows
- Transaction Date
- Cash Flow Category

**Source data:** Synthetic cash flow dataset.

**Aggregation:** Calculate using total inflows and outflows within the selected period.

**Example:**

Cash Inflows = £120,000,000  
Cash Outflows = £115,000,000

Net Cash Flow = £5,000,000.

**Business interpretation:**  
A positive result indicates that cash inflows exceeded cash outflows during the period. A negative result indicates the opposite.

**Reporting considerations:**  
This project uses a simplified management cash flow dataset. It is not intended to replace a formal statement of cash flows or a regulatory liquidity report.

### KPI E04 – Opening Cash Balance

**Definition:**  
The cash balance at the beginning of a reporting period.

**Formula:**

Opening Cash Balance = Previous Period Closing Cash Balance

**Data requirements:**
- Cash Account ID
- Snapshot Date
- Closing Cash Balance

**Source data:** Synthetic cash balance snapshots.

**Aggregation:** Use the opening balance at the start of the selected period. Do not sum daily or monthly opening balances.

**Example:**  
Opening cash balance on 1 April = £50,000,000.

**Business interpretation:**  
Establishes the starting cash position for the reporting period.

### KPI E05 – Closing Cash Balance

**Definition:**  
The cash balance at the end of a reporting period.

**Formula:**

Closing Cash Balance = Opening Cash Balance + Net Cash Flow

**Data requirements:**
- Opening Cash Balance
- Cash Inflows
- Cash Outflows
- Snapshot Date

**Source data:** Synthetic cash flow dataset and cash balance snapshots.

**Aggregation:** Use the closing balance at the end of the selected period. Do not sum balances across dates.

**Example:**

Opening Cash Balance = £50,000,000  
Net Cash Flow = £5,000,000

Closing Cash Balance = £55,000,000.

**Business interpretation:**  
Provides a view of the cash position at the end of the reporting period.

**Reporting considerations:**  
The closing balance must reconcile to the opening balance plus net cash flow, subject to documented adjustments and scope.

## 8. KPI Category F – Forecasting and Planning

Forecasting metrics are derived outputs rather than historical accounting KPIs. Their assumptions and methodology must be documented separately.

### KPI F01 – Forecast Income

**Definition:**  
Estimated future income based on historical performance, portfolio assumptions, pricing and expected business activity.

**Calculation approach:**

Forecast Income = Forecast Interest Income + Forecast Non-interest Income

**Data requirements:**
- Historical Income
- Product Balances
- Interest Rate Assumptions
- Income Growth Assumptions
- Forecast Period
- Scenario

**Source data:** Historical financial data and forecast assumptions.

**Aggregation:** Sum forecast income across products and departments for the selected period.

**Example:**  
Forecast monthly income under the base scenario = £6,600,000.

**Business interpretation:**  
Supports planning and assessment of future income performance.

### KPI F02 – Forecast Operating Expenses

**Definition:**  
Estimated future operating expenditure based on historical costs and planned changes.

**Calculation approach:**

Forecast Operating Expenses = Forecast Personnel Costs + Forecast Technology Costs + Forecast Other Operating Expenses

**Data requirements:**
- Historical Operating Expenses
- Department
- Expense Category
- Inflation Assumptions
- Headcount Assumptions
- Planned Investments
- Forecast Period
- Scenario

**Source data:** Historical General Ledger and forecast assumptions.

**Aggregation:** Sum forecast expenses by reporting period, department and category.

**Example:**  
Forecast monthly operating expenses under the base scenario = £4,100,000.

**Business interpretation:**  
Allows management to identify expected cost pressures and assess future budget requirements.

### KPI F03 – Forecast Operating Profit

**Definition:**  
Estimated operating profit based on forecast income, operating expenses and impairment assumptions.

**Formula:**

Forecast Operating Profit = Forecast Total Income − Forecast Operating Expenses − Forecast Impairment Charges

**Data requirements:**
- Forecast Total Income
- Forecast Operating Expenses
- Forecast Impairment Charges
- Forecast Period
- Scenario

**Source data:** Forecast model.

**Aggregation:** Calculate from aggregated forecast components.

**Example:**

Forecast Total Income = £6,600,000  
Forecast Operating Expenses = £4,100,000  
Forecast Impairment Charges = £150,000

Forecast Operating Profit = £2,350,000.

**Business interpretation:**  
Supports forward-looking financial planning and scenario analysis.

### KPI F04 – Forecast Variance

**Definition:**  
Measures the difference between forecast financial performance and the approved budget or actual performance, depending on the comparison selected.

**Formula:**

Forecast Variance = Forecast Amount − Comparison Amount

**Data requirements:**
- Forecast Amount
- Budget or Actual Amount
- Financial Category
- Forecast Scenario
- Reporting Period

**Source data:** Forecast model, Budget dataset and General Ledger.

**Aggregation:** Calculate from aggregated values.

**Business interpretation:**  
Highlights expected financial performance above or below plan and helps management identify potential issues before the reporting period closes.

## 10. Data Quality and Calculation Controls

The following controls should be implemented during data preparation and validation.

| Control ID | Validation | Expected result |
|---|---|---|
| QC01 | Duplicate transaction IDs | No duplicate transaction IDs |
| QC02 | Missing posting dates | No missing dates in financial transactions |
| QC03 | Missing financial categories | All transactions classified |
| QC04 | Missing department references | Valid department mapping |
| QC05 | Missing product references | Valid product mapping where required |
| QC06 | Invalid financial amounts | No unexpected null or invalid numeric values |
| QC07 | Budget completeness | Budget records available for required periods |
| QC08 | Financial period mapping | Correct April–March financial year |
| QC09 | General Ledger reconciliation | Reporting totals reconcile to source totals |
| QC10 | Cash flow reconciliation | Opening balance + net cash flow = closing balance |
| QC11 | Budget variance | Actual minus budget reconciles |
| QC12 | KPI denominator checks | No division-by-zero errors |
| QC13 | Product cost allocation | Allocated costs reconcile to allocation source totals |
| QC14 | Portfolio snapshot checks | One valid reporting snapshot per product and date |

### 10.1 Reconciliation principles

- General Ledger income and expense totals must reconcile to source data.
- Budget totals must reconcile to the approved budget dataset.
- Product-level income and expense allocations must reconcile to the relevant financial totals where the allocation is intended to be complete.
- Cash movements must reconcile to cash balances within the defined dataset scope.
- KPI calculations must be validated independently using sample calculations.

### 10.2 Missing and zero values

- Missing financial data must not automatically be treated as zero.
- A genuine zero value should remain zero.
- Missing budget values should be flagged rather than silently interpreted as zero.
- Ratios with zero denominators should return blank or not applicable.
- Incomplete reporting periods should be identified to avoid misleading comparisons.

## 11. Reporting and Aggregation Rules

The Power BI model must support the following reporting views:

| Reporting view | Calculation rule |
|---|---|
| Monthly | Transactions within the selected financial month |
| Quarterly | Sum of monthly financial amounts in the quarter |
| Year-to-date | Cumulative values from April to the selected month |
| Full financial year | Sum of all twelve financial periods |
| Year-on-year | Compare equivalent financial periods between years |
| Department | Aggregate transactions by department |
| Product | Aggregate product-level values by product |
| Cost centre | Aggregate transactions by cost centre |

### 11.1 Snapshot measures

Portfolio balances and cash balances are point-in-time measures.

When a user selects a month, the report should use the appropriate reporting date or period-end snapshot.

When a user selects multiple months, the report must use a clearly defined average or closing balance rather than summing snapshots.

### 11.2 Ratio measures

The following measures must be recalculated at every reporting level:

- Net Interest Margin
- Operating Profit Margin
- Cost-to-Income Ratio
- Budget Variance Percentage
- Budget Utilisation
- Product Net Interest Margin

This ensures that the results remain mathematically correct when users filter by month, department or product.

### 11.3 Financial category mapping

Each General Ledger account must be mapped to a standard reporting category.

Recommended categories:

- Interest Income
- Interest Expense
- Non-interest Income
- Personnel Expenses
- Technology Expenses
- Premises Expenses
- Marketing Expenses
- Professional Services
- Other Operating Expenses
- Impairment Charges
- Cash Inflows
- Cash Outflows

The mapping should be maintained in a dedicated dimension or reference table rather than embedded in multiple separate calculations.

## 13. Assumptions and Limitations

The following assumptions apply to the initial project implementation:

1. Northbridge Building Society is a fictional organisation.
2. All financial data will be synthetic.
3. Financial reporting is based on a monthly April–March financial year.
4. General Ledger transactions are the primary source for historical income and expenditure.
5. Budget figures represent approved monthly departmental or cost-centre allocations.
6. Mortgage and savings balances are provided as periodic snapshots.
7. Product profitability is based initially on product income and directly attributable costs.
8. Any allocation of funding costs or overheads must be documented.
9. Cash flow reporting is a simplified management reporting exercise and not a regulatory liquidity model.
10. Forecasting assumptions will be documented separately and clearly distinguished from actual financial results.
11. The project does not attempt to implement full IFRS reporting or regulatory capital calculations.
