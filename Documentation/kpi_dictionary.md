# Northbridge Building Society
## KPI Dictionary & Financial Metric Framework

**Project:** Financial Performance & Profitability Intelligence  
**Project ID:** FPI-001  
**Document Version:** 2.0  
**Currency:** GBP (£)  
**Financial Year:** April – March

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the key performance indicators used in the Northbridge Building Society financial reporting solution.

The KPI framework provides consistent definitions and calculation methods across the three Power BI dashboards:

1. Financial Overview.
2. Budget vs Actual.
3. Product Performance.

All metrics and examples are designed for a fictional building society using synthetic financial data.

## 2. General Calculation Principles

The following rules apply across the reporting solution.

| Principle | Definition |
|---|---|
| Currency | All monetary values are represented in GBP (£). |
| Reporting frequency | Financial performance is reported monthly, with annual and year-to-date analysis where appropriate. |
| Financial year | April to March. |
| Income | Income is presented as a positive value. |
| Expenses | Expenses are presented as a positive value for management reporting. |
| Profit | Calculated as income less expenses. |
| Budget variance | Calculated as actual less budget. Its interpretation depends on whether the metric is income or expenditure. |
| Portfolio balances | Mortgage and savings balances represent snapshots at a reporting date, not monthly transactions. |
| Ratios | Ratios must be recalculated using aggregated numerator and denominator values, rather than averaging monthly percentages. |

## 3. Financial Performance KPIs

### KPI01 — Total Income

**Definition:** Total income generated by the organisation during the reporting period.

**Calculation:**

`Total Income = Interest Income + Non-interest Income`

**Purpose:** Measures the organisation's overall income generation.

**Analysis:** Monthly trends, annual performance and income composition.

### KPI02 — Net Interest Income (NII)

**Definition:** The difference between interest earned on assets and interest paid on funding and deposits.

**Calculation:**

`NII = Interest Income - Interest Expense`

**Purpose:** Measures the organisation's core interest-based income.

**Analysis:** Changes in mortgage income, savings interest costs and overall interest performance.

### KPI03 — Total Operating Expenses

**Definition:** The total costs incurred from operating the organisation during the reporting period.

**Calculation:**

`Total Operating Expenses = Sum of Operating Expense Transactions`

Examples include:

- Personnel costs.
- Technology costs.
- Property and facilities.
- Marketing expenditure.
- Professional services.
- Other operating costs.

**Purpose:** Monitor the organisation's cost base.

### KPI04 — Operating Profit

**Definition:** The difference between total income and operating expenses.

**Calculation:**

`Operating Profit = Total Income - Total Operating Expenses`

**Purpose:** Measures the organisation's operating financial performance before any additional items excluded from the simplified management reporting definition.

### KPI05 — Operating Profit Margin

**Definition:** The percentage of total income remaining after operating expenses.

**Calculation:**

`Operating Profit Margin = Operating Profit / Total Income`

**Purpose:** Measures operating profitability relative to income.

**Format:** Percentage.

### KPI06 — Net Interest Margin (NIM)

**Definition:** Measures net interest income relative to average interest-earning assets.

**Calculation:**

`NIM = Annualised Net Interest Income / Average Interest-Earning Assets`

**Purpose:** Measures the effectiveness of interest-generating assets.

**Important:** For monthly reporting, annualise the monthly net interest income by multiplying by 12. For a full financial year, use the annual net interest income without further annualisation.

The denominator should represent average interest-earning assets, not total liabilities or savings deposits.

For this project, the mortgage portfolio may be used as a simplified proxy for interest-earning assets, provided the limitation is clearly disclosed.

**Format:** Percentage.

### KPI07 — Cost-to-Income Ratio (CIR)

**Definition:** Measures operating expenses relative to total income.

**Calculation:**

`CIR = Total Operating Expenses / Total Income`

**Purpose:** Measures the cost efficiency of the organisation.

**Format:** Percentage.

## 4. Budget Management KPIs

### KPI08 — Budget

**Definition:** The approved financial allocation for a department, cost centre or financial category during a reporting period.

**Calculation:**

`Budget = Sum of Approved Budget Amounts`

**Purpose:** Establish the financial baseline for performance monitoring.

### KPI09 — Actual

**Definition:** The actual financial amount recorded during the reporting period.

**Calculation:**

`Actual = Sum of Relevant General Ledger Amounts`

**Purpose:** Measure actual financial performance or expenditure.

### KPI10 — Budget Variance

**Definition:** The difference between actual financial performance and the approved budget.

**Calculation:**

`Budget Variance = Actual - Budget`

**Interpretation:**

- For income, a positive variance is generally favourable.
- For expenses, a positive variance is generally unfavourable.
- A negative variance has the opposite interpretation.

**Purpose:** Identify financial areas performing above or below budget.

### KPI11 — Budget Variance %

**Definition:** Measures the budget variance relative to the approved budget.

**Calculation:**

`Budget Variance % = (Actual - Budget) / Budget`

**Purpose:** Allows comparison of financial variances across departments and expense categories.

**Format:** Percentage.

If the budget is zero, the percentage should return blank or be handled separately rather than producing an invalid result.

## 5. Product Performance KPIs

### KPI12 — Mortgage Balance

**Definition:** The outstanding balance of the mortgage portfolio at a specific reporting date.

**Calculation:**

`Mortgage Balance = Sum of Mortgage Product Balances`

**Purpose:** Monitor the size and development of the mortgage portfolio.

**Important:** This is a reporting-date snapshot and must not be added together across different months.

### KPI13 — Savings Balance

**Definition:** The total savings deposits held by the organisation at a specific reporting date.

**Calculation:**

`Savings Balance = Sum of Savings Product Balances`

**Purpose:** Monitor the size and development of the savings portfolio.

**Important:** This is a reporting-date snapshot and must not be added together across different months.

### KPI14 — Product Income

**Definition:** Income attributed to an individual mortgage or savings product during the reporting period.

**Calculation:**

`Product Income = Sum of Product-Attributed Income`

**Purpose:** Compare income generation across different products.

**Important:** Product attribution must be available in the underlying financial data. Income that cannot be reliably attributed to a product should remain unallocated.

### KPI15 — Product Contribution

**Definition:** The income generated by a product after deducting directly attributable costs.

**Calculation:**

`Product Contribution = Product Income - Directly Attributable Product Costs`

**Purpose:** Assess the financial contribution of individual products.

**Important:** This metric is only meaningful where costs can be reliably attributed to products. It should not be presented as fully allocated product profitability if shared operating costs have not been allocated.

Product contribution is different from Net Interest Income. It may include non-interest income and directly attributable costs.

## 6. Data Requirements

The following data is required to calculate the KPIs.

| Data source | Required information | Main purpose |
|---|---|---|
| General Ledger | Transaction date, account, department, amount and product where applicable | Income and expense reporting |
| Budget | Reporting month, department, financial category, approved budget and version | Budget comparisons |
| Product Snapshots | Reporting date, product, product type and outstanding balance | Mortgage and savings analysis |
| Department | Department name and relevant organisational information | Departmental reporting |
| Product | Product name, product type and relevant classification | Product analysis |

## 7. Financial Reporting Rules

### 7.1 Income and Expense Classification

Each general ledger account must be mapped to an appropriate financial category.

For example:

| Account description | Financial category | Reporting treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Mortgage Interest Income | Interest Income | Income |
| Savings Interest Expense | Interest Expense | Expense |
| Account Fees | Non-interest Income | Income |
| Employee Salaries | Personnel Costs | Expense |
| Software Licences | Technology Costs | Expense |
| Office Rent | Property Costs | Expense |

### 7.2 Budget Variance Interpretation

Budget variance must be interpreted according to the financial category.

Example:

| Category | Budget | Actual | Variance | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Income | £100,000 | £110,000 | +£10,000 | Favourable |
| Expenses | £100,000 | £110,000 | +£10,000 | Unfavourable |
| Expenses | £100,000 | £90,000 | -£10,000 | Favourable |

### 7.3 Aggregation Rules

- Monetary transaction values can be summed across the relevant reporting period.
- Mortgage and savings balances must be treated as snapshots.
- Annual ratios must be recalculated using annual aggregated values.
- Budget comparisons must use the same reporting period and financial category.
- Product contribution must only include costs with a documented attribution method.

## 8. Data Quality and Validation

The following checks will be applied during data preparation and reporting.

| ID | Validation check |
|---|---|
| QC01 | Financial transactions must contain a valid transaction date. |
| QC02 | General ledger transactions must reference a valid account. |
| QC03 | Financial amounts must be numeric and valid. |
| QC04 | Every financial account must map to a reporting category. |
| QC05 | Budget records must contain a valid reporting period and department. |
| QC06 | Budget records must not contain duplicate records at the defined budget grain. |
| QC07 | Product snapshots must contain a valid product and reporting date. |
| QC08 | Product balances must be non-negative unless an explicitly documented exception exists. |
| QC09 | Financial totals must reconcile between source data and the reporting model. |
| QC10 | Budget and actual figures must use consistent financial categories and reporting periods. |

## 9. Assumptions and Limitations

- All financial data is synthetic and illustrative.
- The project uses simplified management reporting definitions.
- Total income and operating expenses are defined according to the project's financial category mapping.
- The simplified NIM calculation may use the mortgage portfolio as a proxy for interest-earning assets.
- Product income and direct costs may not be fully attributable to every product.
- Product contribution should not be interpreted as fully allocated net profitability.
- Cash flow, forecasting and regulatory financial metrics are outside the initial KPI framework.
